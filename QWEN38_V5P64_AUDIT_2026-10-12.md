## P0: Circuit grading returned infrastructure failures as training rewards

### Exact failure chain

1. `.science/qwen-comparison-20261006/od_deploy.py` selects `problem='qubit'` for every farm, reads an existing qubit training task's `run` script and environment, and changes the profile/bundle references.
2. The farm profiles advertise math, routing, and placement grading. Their archives contain placement source and inputs, but the reused qubit startup script only runs routing/CPU preparation.
3. `prepare_cpu_host.sh` creates `.science/venv` with Python 3.11 and writes generic `.science/ready.json`. It does not create placement's Python 3.12 `.science/candidate-venv` or the separate placement grader environment.
4. `GradingService._warmup` executes a real reference grade for math, but for routing/placement only checks whether `.science/ready.json` exists. The routing marker incorrectly qualifies placement as ready.
5. `placement_task.evaluate` calls the absent `.science/candidate-venv/bin/python`.
6. `placement_task.main` catches every exception and returns `invalid(...)`, including infrastructure setup errors. The farm reports a completed request with reward zero instead of raising an infrastructure failure that aborts/retries the training batch.
7. The transport uses the fast failure completion time in its performance estimate. This creates an additional risk of preferring a broken pool because it appears fast.

This is a deterministic launch/configuration defect, not evidence of spot preemption or a venv disappearing after cleanup. All four stable farms have the routing `ready.json`, routing venv, and placement inputs, but no candidate venv. The trainer's actual placement runtime does have its candidate venv.

The standard `tpu/science/package_training.py` already generates both preparations for a multi-family farm, with `SCIENCE_PLACEMENT_VENV=venv-placement` to avoid conflicting dependency locks. The custom on-demand launch path bypassed that generated command. A bundle checksum verified bytes, not completeness of the startup procedure.

## P1: Recovery has fixed availability, not validated correctness

- The earlier disk repair selected checkpoint 5 for Circuit because its archive was complete. This proves storage durability, not trustworthy rewards. It is not the appropriate boundary for the newly discovered grading defect.
- Repeated exact-bundle modifications, profile substitutions, source overlays, and script reuse make it difficult to reason about the full executable deployment. The launch script needs to be versioned and verified alongside the archive and effective profile.
- A restart from a clean checkpoint must restore matching model/optimizer/search/step state. It is ordinary recovery, not a new Gen-1 branch: do not reset the optimizer or PUCT counts.
- Prevent a failed checkpoint/sampler export from silently keeping an older sampling adapter while the global step advances. The inspected ensemble `_refresh_or_keep` catches export errors and keeps the old client; step commit/recovery consistency needs an explicit gate.
- Recovery artifacts and databases should remain untouched until the rollback boundary and restore procedure are validated.

## P1: Disk failure and cleanup

The stable machine has roughly 90 GiB boot filesystems; RAM-backed model/compilation caches do not move checkpoint archives, adapter uploads, environment trees, or DB snapshots into RAM. Old checkpoints and transient exports exhausted the boot disks and interrupted saves/writeback.

The prior repair removed 43.16 GiB of verified cloud-backed older archives/derived exports across the trainers. Circuit/AC1 now include active-run checkpoint retention and retired-upload cleanup. This audit found approximately 13.8 GiB free on each active trainer and 17–20 GiB on farms. These are improved snapshots, not evidence of a sustainable bound over 15 steps.

Required gate: demonstrate bounded disk use through at least two save/upload/writeback cycles, verify latest and prior recovery points remotely, and monitor both bytes and inodes. Account separately for grader artifacts, logs, SQLite snapshots, immutable runtime copies, active and retired uploads, and checkpoint archives. Do not delete a file just because a run is old or an object of the same name exists remotely.

## P1: Grading capacity exists but allocation is uneven

Circuit did dispatch grading to all three stable farms. In the current sampled phase, 7,407 remote case completions versus 300 local completions were observed, but remote completions are broken grades. Their seconds-long median is not a useful performance benchmark.

Local Circuit cases had median end-to-end transport time about 20 minutes and maximum about 38 minutes. Those measurements include queueing; they do not prove the candidate violated its 310-second per-case budget.

AC1 discovered both stable `10.128.1.90` and v5p-32 `10.202.0.135`. The latter advertises 256 math slots and was healthy, but this phase dispatched zero grades there. Stable farm and trainer received the work. The scheduler minimizes estimated waiting backlog, then prefers local, then sorts by pool name; it does not balance idle pools by their fraction of capacity used or predict full completion time. That policy can keep choosing the first stable farm while the v5p-32 remains idle. This is a code-supported explanation; the live scheduler's full per-decision state is not instrumented, so it is not a fully reproduced diagnosis yet.

Required gate: deterministic routing replay plus real per-pool started/completed/queued metrics. Validate successful grades before using latency to steer more work toward a pool. Distinguish transport completion, candidate execution, correctness, infrastructure failures, and per-case/per-program counts.

## P1: Farm discovery and deployed capacity differ from intended capacity

- Stable farms were statically assigned one per trainer. Finishing Erdős/Qubit did not automatically advertise their farms to Circuit. Circuit's candidate list was manually expanded to three and that change persisted in the registry.
- Circuit and AC1 registrations currently allow the v5p pool by default; only Qubit's registration explicitly also allows East5 v6e-8. As a result, the healthy unleased two-engine Qwen3.8 v6e-8 job 2371 is not offered to those two trainers. This is an admission/configuration gap, not a model mismatch.
- Live v5p-32 Qwen3.8 job 2137 is four engines at 16 sequences, leased to AC1. It has not received the handoff's 64-sequence upgrade. Another agent owns farm rebuilding; do not cancel or replace its services as part of this audit.
- Three Asia Qwen3.8 farm jobs were pending/recovering; they are not usable capacity in this snapshot.
- Up to three leased workers does not mean three ready engines or useful completion throughput. Discovery should expose compatible, actually ready capacity and avoid indefinitely reserving unused workers after runs finish.

## P2: Why step times remain large

Recent completed Circuit steps: 181, 173, and 250 minutes; AC1 steps 6–8: approximately 174–177 minutes. These are historical measured wall times, not predictions for the repaired design.

Generation, grading, and streamed backward passes overlap. The metric `time/sampling` includes waiting for graded groups; `qwen/time/train` is the remaining backward/optimizer drain after that phase, not total TPU training compute. The full step time includes both plus checkpoint/export overhead.

The current Circuit phase produced all 512 rollouts in approximately 54 minutes, with two farms added partway through. Single-host backward tiles are about 21 seconds at shape `[4,22528]`; a 117-tile block alone takes about 42 minutes. Extra inference farms do not shorten these backward passes. AC1's observed generation completed in approximately 54 minutes after dispatch, but slow grades and queued training work remain afterward.

Do not infer a 3x step speedup from 3x inference concurrency. Do not publish a new finish ETA until an entirely validated step has completed under the final allocation. Training packing was previously piloted separately; it should not be combined with the correctness repair or declared safe from throughput alone.

## Proposed recovery sequence and acceptance gates

1. **Contain and preserve (proposed, not authorized under the current audit-only instruction).** Before a future repair/relaunch, stop Circuit and preserve logs, trajectory files, full checkpoint ledger, relevant database snapshot, search snapshots, and bundle/script hashes. If a later maintenance window includes AC1, stop it as well; do not delete the stable TPU or interrupt the other agent's farm rebuild. No new experiment launches.
2. **One reviewed deployment path.** Generate runtime preparation from the actual multi-family profile using the standard science packager. Use the same startup path on assigned stable hosts as on managed farms. Freeze source, overlay, profile, task script and dependency manifests together. Remove the qubit-script substitution rather than patching only today's missing directory.
3. **Make infrastructure failures impossible to learn from.** Validate candidate interpreter, executable/version, pinned dependencies, input suite and trusted grader before serving placement. Run a known-good placement candidate on every serving host through the real lease/HTTP/Ray/cgroup path; verify the expected score. Missing executable, missing inputs, broken imports and grader failure must raise typed infrastructure errors and prevent optimizer/checkpoint advancement. Keep legitimate candidate errors as zero rewards. Negative tests must deliberately remove each dependency and prove batch abort.
4. **Reconcile Main-infer deliberately.** Add the missing refresh-flood fix while preserving the trainer-specific bounded transport and disk fixes. Compare functional behavior, not only commit ancestry. Let the farm owner complete the Qwen3.8 64-sequence rebuild, then verify the running capabilities and actual useful generation; do not assume a changed profile redeployed a job. Do not adopt eager/subset selection in this matched RL experiment.
5. **Validate recovery before replay.** Download/verify Circuit checkpoint 2 from Central1 on the Central1 trainer; restore its optimizer and exact step-2 search tree; regenerate and attest its sampling adapter. Validate restored logits/checkpoint semantics and run actual grading references. Use a clearly identified corrected run/attempt so contaminated steps cannot be mistaken for clean comparison data. Audit Qubit worker-exit messages separately; do not discard valid completed results without evidence.
6. **One complete acceptance step.** Run Circuit update 3 with all 16 groups x32 rollouts, all 17 cases per eligible program, unchanged PWC/seed/thinking budgets. Verify expected case counts, zero infrastructure-as-reward events, meaningful gradients, completed optimizer update, model and sampler export, matching search snapshot and Central1 writeback. Demonstrate local/farm replay agreement on representative candidates, including invalid and timeout cases.
7. **Recovery and endurance check.** From that committed state, exercise a controlled client restart and a farm-loss/lease-refresh scenario without changing checkpoint/search identity or duplicating rewards. Then complete a second full step and verify disk use, finalization, re-leasing and discovery. Promote AC1 after its grading scheduler/capacity behavior is understood; retain existing proven weights/search state.
8. **Measure before expanding.** Report generation completion, case/program grading service and queue time, trainer compute/drain, save/upload time, useful tokens/s, successful grades/s and step wall time. Only then consider training packing, more farm leases or further concurrency. No numerical-comparison changes hidden inside a performance fix.
